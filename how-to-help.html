How to help

Help that reaches the right people

Wanting to help is the first step, but how you help matters just as much. In every major emergency, some donations are lost to fake charities and look-alike websites: the giver loses their money, and the people in crisis never receive the help meant for them. Knowing how to give safely protects both.

That is why we built this website, and this page in particular. Here you will find a simple safe-giving checklist, a list of well-known official aid organizations around the world, how we check every link on this site, and how to tell us about anything that looks wrong.

Before you donate

Give safely

Most appeals for help are genuine, but scammers copy real charities' names, logos and photos, especially right after a disaster. A few simple checks before you give make it much harder for them, and make sure your money goes where you meant it to.

1Part one

Safe-giving checklist

Run through these five checks before every donation. They take a few minutes and stop most scams.

  1. Go to the website yourselfType the address or use a bookmark. Don't follow donation links in unexpected messages or posts.
  2. Check the exact name and web addressFake charities use names and addresses that only look like real ones.
  3. Look it up in an official registerUse your country's charity register, or a charity evaluator such as Charity Navigator or BBB Wise Giving Alliance.
  4. Pay safelyUse a credit card or check. Never pay by gift card, wire transfer or cryptocurrency.
  5. Don't let anyone rush youA real charity will welcome your donation at any time.
